# Research-field admission

Read this reference whenever the requested direction may be a job, tool, product, broad interest, or otherwise not yet a defensible research field.

## Admission test

Classify the direction as:

- **Accept:** It names a coherent field with a durable scholarly or industrial research community, foundational literature, characteristic methods, recognized evaluation venues or institutions, and open questions supporting original contributions.
- **Reframe:** It names a job, tool, framework, product, stack, certification, or miscellaneous skill bundle. Preserve the learner's motivation but reject that label as the doctoral target.
- **Narrow:** It is too broad to support one defensible literature review and research agenda.

Do not invent a scholarly-sounding hybrid label merely to pass this test.

## Candidate construction

For a reframed or narrowed request:

1. Identify the phenomena and research problems motivating the learner.
2. Map them to two to four recognized, meaningfully distinct fields.
3. Establish each candidate with its communities, venues, methods, or canonical problem families.
4. Compare research object, characteristic questions, methods, expected research practice, and connection to the original motivation.
5. Ask the learner to choose. Do not select, recommend, rank, or silently default.
6. Stop. Do not design the review, select literature, or generate the tutorial workspace until the learner explicitly chooses.
7. After selection, record the original wording, admission decision, chosen field, rationale, recognition evidence, and adjacent fields.

The skill owns candidate discovery and qualification; the learner owns the final research identity. Do not delegate normalization by merely asking “which research field do you mean?” Do not reject the learner's ambition; reject only the invalid framing and replace it constructively.

Example only: “become a frontend engineer” is not an admissible terminal field. Depending on the learner's motivation, plausible candidates could include human-computer interaction, browser and Web-platform systems, programming languages, computer graphics, performance engineering, or accessible computing. Derive actual candidates afresh; this example is neither a taxonomy nor a preferred mapping.

## Response contract

While the field is unresolved, output only:

1. The admission decision and why the original framing is insufficient.
2. Two to four legitimate candidate fields.
3. For each: recognition basis, research object, representative questions, characteristic methods, research practice, and connection to the learner's motivation.
4. A direct request for the learner to choose one candidate.
