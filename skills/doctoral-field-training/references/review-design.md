# Review collection design

Read this reference before planning a new collection or generating a chapter.

## Scope and doctoral outcome

State the admission decision and recognition basis, central review question or thesis, inclusion and exclusion boundaries, historical endpoints, source-selection and verification method, prerequisites, and adjacent fields. Split an overly broad field into defensible tracks rather than hiding breadth inside a long reading list.

Define an observable end state in which the learner can:

1. Reconstruct the field's development and consequential decisions.
2. Critically synthesize primary and secondary literature.
3. Work directly with the field's primary artifacts and methods.
4. Identify, execute, evaluate, and defend a credible frontier contribution.

Make the pacing philosophy explicit. Slow down wherever the learner cannot retrieve, explain, compare, reproduce, or use an idea. Prefer a few connected ideas examined through evidence over compressed coverage. Weeks or months can be orientation or apprenticeship, never doctoral mastery.

## Problem genealogy and literature

Organize the field by causal transitions:

```text
historical situation
→ central problem
→ prior approach
→ binding limitation
→ conceptual or technical turn
→ evidence and adoption
→ limitations and controversy
→ downstream lineage
→ open research problem
```

Periodize by changes in problems, assumptions, methods, or explanatory power—not dates alone.

For each major transition, select a compact cluster: a predecessor or baseline; a turning-point work; a refinement, competing school, or critique; and a modern synthesis when available. Record each work's problem, contribution, method, evidence, assumptions, limitations, downstream influence, and role in the collection's argument. Label confidence as `verified`, `canonical-but-unverified-in-this-session`, or `candidate`.

## Collection architecture

Plan the complete trajectory at initialization in this order, but generate only Chapter 1:

1. Title and doctoral outcome.
2. Field-admission statement.
3. Abstract.
4. Scope, review question, and thesis.
5. Review method and source-selection criteria.
6. Prerequisite and terminology map.
7. Historical periodization and problem-lineage overview.
8. Chronologically ordered analytical chapters.
9. Cross-period synthesis.
10. Current frontier and open problems.
11. Research agenda with candidate questions.
12. Doctoral capability rubric and assessment plan.
13. Verified bibliography with confidence labels.

The table of contents is the curriculum outline; do not create a second week-by-week or course-module outline. Chapters must form one causal history, stand alone as compact review papers, and advance the global thesis. Follow the exact chapter data contract in `tutorial-contract.md`.

For mature software systems, connect each historical problem and design alternative to surviving architecture, specifications, source subsystems, tests, performance evidence, design records, issues, and commits. Progress from guided source archaeology toward independent modification and original contribution; public APIs alone are insufficient.

## Learning design

Embed the following in the review articles rather than replacing the artifact with conversational coaching:

- Retrieval before explanation and spaced return to earlier transitions.
- Interleaving of neighboring methods or schools.
- Self-explanation of why transitions mattered.
- Worked examples with fading scaffolds.
- Reproduction or re-analysis when the field permits it.
- Synthesis writing, oral-defense prompts, and confidence calibration against performance.

Design chapters for patient return, not one-pass consumption. Make tasks expose the difference between familiarity and usable knowledge. Reading acknowledgement controls navigation only; mastery requires observable explanation, comparison, critique, reproduction, synthesis, or application. Cultivate continued learning through earned comprehension, agency, and intellectual continuity—not streaks, points, urgency, artificial scarcity, or escalating notifications.
