# Learning-science reference for Review-Driven Learning

Use this reference when explaining why the skill teaches through paper review, historical lineage, active recall, and incremental mastery.

## Core learning principles

### Retrieval practice / practice testing

Learners remember and transfer knowledge better when they actively retrieve it instead of only rereading. Use low-stakes questions, flashcards, practice problems, oral defense, and “answer before explanation” prompts.

Design implication for this skill:

- Before explaining a chapter, ask the learner what they already think happened and why.
- After a paper cluster, require a closed-book reconstruction of the problem lineage.
- Convert important concepts into later review prompts.

Representative sources:

- Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning. *Psychological Science*, 17(3), 249–255.
- Adesope, O. O., Trevisan, D. A., & Sundararajan, N. (2017). Rethinking the use of tests: A meta-analysis of practice testing. *Review of Educational Research*, 87(3), 659–701.
- Agarwal, P. K., Nunes, L. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2021). Retrieval practice consistently benefits student learning: A systematic review of applied research in schools and classrooms. *Educational Psychology Review*, 33, 1409–1453.

### Spaced / distributed practice

Learning improves when study and review are distributed over time rather than massed in one session.

Design implication for this skill:

- Revisit old paradigm shifts after later modules.
- Maintain a review queue of concepts, paper contributions, and debates.
- Ask the learner to compare old and new methods after a delay.

Representative sources:

- Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. *Psychological Bulletin*, 132(3), 354–380.
- Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques: Promising directions from cognitive and educational psychology. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 14(1), 4–58.

### Interleaving

Mixed practice can help learners discriminate similar concepts, methods, schools, or problem types. It is especially useful when the learner must decide which method applies.

Design implication for this skill:

- Compare neighboring schools or methods, not only one at a time.
- Ask “which historical problem does this paper belong to?”
- Mix predecessor, turning-point, and critique papers.

Representative source:

- Dunlosky et al. (2013), which rates interleaved practice as moderate utility and context-dependent.

### Self-explanation and elaboration

Learners deepen understanding when they explain why a statement is true, why an approach works, how it relates to prior knowledge, and what assumptions it makes.

Design implication for this skill:

- Require “why did this matter?” explanations.
- Ask learners to write causal links between papers.
- Challenge vague summaries that do not identify the prior limitation and downstream impact.

Representative source:

- Dunlosky et al. (2013), which discusses elaborative interrogation and self-explanation as useful but implementation-dependent methods.

### Cognitive load theory

Working memory is limited. Instruction should reduce unnecessary load, sequence complexity, and use worked examples before independent performance.

Design implication for this skill:

- Start each field with a map and vocabulary, not a full literature dump.
- Teach one historical turn at a time.
- Use worked-example review analyses, then partial matrices, then independent review writing.

Representative sources:

- Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning. *Cognitive Science*, 12(2), 257–285.
- Sweller, J., van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Paas, F. (1998). Cognitive architecture and instructional design. *Educational Psychology Review*, 10, 251–296.

### Generative learning

Learners understand better when they actively generate meaning: summarizing, mapping, drawing, imagining, self-testing, self-explaining, teaching, and enacting.

Design implication for this skill:

- Ask for mini reviews, concept maps, debate memos, and teaching explanations.
- Use paper matrices as generative artifacts, not just summaries.

Representative source:

- Fiorella, L., & Mayer, R. E. (2015). *Learning as a Generative Activity: Eight Learning Strategies that Promote Understanding*. Cambridge University Press.

### Self-regulated learning and metacognition

Advanced learning requires planning, monitoring, strategy choice, confidence calibration, and reflection.

Design implication for this skill:

- Ask the learner to state goals and confidence before a module.
- Compare confidence with performance after recall or critique.
- Maintain a “known / shaky / unknown” map.

Representative sources:

- Zimmerman, B. J. (2002). Becoming a self-regulated learner: An overview. *Theory Into Practice*, 41(2), 64–70.
- Theobald, M. (2021). Self-regulated learning training programs enhance university students' academic performance, self-regulated learning strategies, and motivation: A meta-analysis. *Contemporary Educational Psychology*, 66, 101976.

## Why literature review is a good core format

A strong literature review forces the learner to reconstruct:

- The field's central problems.
- The sequence of attempts to solve them.
- The reason each method or theory became necessary.
- The evidence behind each claim.
- The limits and blind spots of each paradigm.
- Open questions and possible research contributions.

This aligns well with doctoral-level learning because doctoral competence is not just recall. It is the ability to judge claims, situate work historically, compare methods, formulate questions, and write original synthesis.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- Do not equate “more history” with “more mastery.” Use history to understand problem evolution.
- Do not produce paper-summary lists without synthesis.
- Do not use learning styles as a core personalization theory; evidence for matching instruction to visual/auditory/etc. styles is weak.
- Do not make growth mindset the main mechanism for achievement gains; evidence is mixed and effects are often small.
- Do not skip practice and critique. Reading review-like content is not enough.
